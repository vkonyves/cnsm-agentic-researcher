### 4A. Informativeness and intervention-activation requirement

The submitted predecessor study demonstrated that a technically correct preregistered experiment can nevertheless be scientifically weak when the intervention is almost never activated. The follow-up must therefore distinguish **design validity** from **design informativeness**.

Do not optimize for statistical significance, a positive result, or a preferred direction of effect. Instead, optimize the pre-confirmatory design for the ability to estimate the scientific quantity it claims to study.

If a proposed intervention can affect the outcome only when a trigger event occurs — for example, a validator/repair pipeline can improve a configuration only when the initial configuration contains a detectable actionable defect — then before final confirmatory execution the autonomous researcher must ensure that the design provides a scientifically defensible opportunity to observe such trigger events.

Acceptable mechanisms include, when supported by the execution capabilities:

- a disjoint pre-confirmatory calibration/pilot stage over a prespecified difficulty spectrum;
- deterministic task-difficulty stratification selected by a rule frozen before confirmatory outcomes;
- a preregistered controlled-fault or controlled-challenge experiment with fault operators frozen before outcomes;
- or another outcome-independent design that provides sufficient intervention opportunities for meaningful estimation.

Calibration is **not confirmatory evidence**. Calibration tasks, model outputs, and outcomes must be disjoint from the final confirmatory task set and must never be pooled into confirmatory estimates.

The autonomous researcher must define, before confirmatory execution:

1. what event activates the intervention;
2. what quantity of activation/discordance is needed for useful precision or power;
3. how the calibration or challenge mechanism is selected without using confirmatory outcomes;
4. the exact rule that freezes the final task/difficulty distribution;
5. the fresh held-out confirmatory task-generation rule;
6. the confirmatory sample-size justification;
7. stopping rules that do not depend on obtaining statistical significance.

Reject a candidate design as insufficiently informative if, based on pre-confirmatory evidence or structural reasoning, the intervention is likely to remain effectively unexercised and no scientifically valid calibration/challenge mechanism is available.

A successful study may still produce a null or unfavorable result. The requirement is that the intervention was meaningfully testable, not that the effect be statistically significant.
